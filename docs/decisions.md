# Design decisions

An append-only record of the design choices in this repository, the measured evidence behind each, and what was rejected. Entries are dated by when the decision landed in git, with the commit cited. Every number is copied from a committed results file, test or script output, and the source is named. Nothing is quoted from memory. Entries are never rewritten: a changed decision gets a new entry, and the old entry's **Status** line alone is updated to point at it. Corrections stay visible beside what they correct.

Same format and rules as `wrist-ppg-motion-robust-hr` and `cgm-forecast-conformal`; the mechanical guard (`tests/test_decision_log.py`) is in place from the first entry.

---

### D-001 · The target: Bent et al. 2021 on the BIG IDEAs Lab data, and why the alternatives were not chosen
- **Date / commit:** 2026-10-20 · `a64b4ec`
- **Status:** adopted
- **Decision:** the paper to reproduce is Bent, Cho, Henriquez et al. (2021), *Engineering digital biomarkers of interstitial glucose from noninvasive smartwatches*, npj Digital Medicine 4:89, on the BIG IDEAs Lab Glycemic Variability and Wearable Device Data (PhysioNet v1.1.3): 16 participants, 8–10 days each, Empatica E4 (BVP 64 Hz, ACC 32 Hz, EDA and TEMP 4 Hz, derived HR and IBI) with Dexcom G6 every five minutes and a food log. The data are public under ODC-BY v1.0 and the companion code is public (Duke DBDP).
- **Rejected:** Zhang et al. — no data release; the institution withheld it. Bhatia et al. — the paper makes no claim specific enough to reproduce.
- **The reproduction's value, stated before anything is fitted:** the baselines the paper did not report (population mean, per-participant mean, persistence where lagged glucose is used, a per-participant linear model) and the split it did; both are pre-registered in `docs/protocol.md` §8–9. If the paper's split is random within participant, the gap to a time-blocked split is the headline. The repository name `nir-glucose-reproduction` predates the choice and stays until the write-up decides otherwise.

### D-002 · The licence, and the repository's own stricter policy
- **Date / commit:** 2026-10-20 · `a64b4ec`
- **Status:** adopted
- **Decision:** the data are under the Open Data Commons Attribution Licence v1.0 — attribution required, redistribution permitted — with PhysioNet's two required citations recorded in `data/README.md`. The licence would permit derived data and traces in this repository; **the repository's policy from repo 2 holds anyway**: no raw data, no caches, no per-participant time series in a figure. The participants are time-shifted individuals, and the policy costs nothing the reproduction needs. Raw files live outside the tracked tree (`data/` is gitignored but for its README), the cache at `~/.cache/nir-glucose-reproduction`.

### D-003 · Two split regimes, always reported side by side
- **Date / commit:** 2026-10-20 · `a64b4ec`
- **Status:** superseded by D-009 (three regimes R1–R3; the time-blocked split gains a 24-h exclusion zone)
- **Decision:** every result is reported under the paper's split, exactly as the protocol records it, and under a time-blocked split — train on the first part of each participant's record, test on the last, no test window sharing time with its training set — with the fraction fixed before the first fit (`docs/protocol.md` §9). Neither regime is dropped because the other looks better. The reason is the one a wearable reviewer would give: a random split of windows within a participant lets a model learn the participant's glucose trajectory and read it back, and that is not sensing.

### D-004 · The baselines the paper did not report, pre-registered
- **Date / commit:** 2026-10-20 · `a64b4ec`
- **Status:** superseded by D-009 (persistence dropped — no model uses lagged glucose; B2 added)
- **Decision:** population mean; per-participant mean over the training period only; persistence for any model that uses lagged glucose; a per-participant linear model on the same features — all under both regimes, per participant with n and pooled, before any published model is fitted (`docs/protocol.md` §8). A published model that does not beat the per-participant mean under the paper's split, or the linear model under the time-blocked split, has not demonstrated sensing. The clinical scorer and the iCGM analogue module are vendored from `cgm-forecast-conformal` with their tests, not rewritten; they carry the commit they came from.

### D-005 · Open — the E4 and Dexcom facts to re-verify before the loader is trusted
- **Date / commit:** 2026-10-20 · `2742ee6`
- **Status:** closed 2026-10-20 by the measurements below (same entry)
- **Note:** the facts the loader must assert rather than assume, each with the lesson it comes from: E4 accelerometer CSVs are in units of 1/64 g (the PhysioNet readme says so; in `wrist-ppg-motion-robust-hr` a brief said the same and the data were already in g — so it is measured here: median magnitude ≈ 64 before scaling, ≈ 1.0 g after); BVP is zero-centred with the DC removed (mean against SD on a resting hour; perfusion index unavailable, as in repo 1); `HR.csv` and `IBI.csv` are Empatica-derived, not raw, and whether Bent et al. used them is a protocol question; timestamps are date-shifted per participant and the shift must be consistent across a participant's E4 and Dexcom files (overlap asserted); Dexcom G6 values are in mg/dL and censored to 40–400 (floored and ceilinged readings counted, as in repo 2). Each becomes a measured number in the entry that closes this one.
- **Measured** (`results/coverage.csv`, all 16 participants, after every one of the 130 files verified against `SHA256SUMS.txt`): **ACC median raw magnitude 62.7–65.4 across participants — the files are in 1/64 g, and after scaling the median is 0.98–1.02 g.** BVP on each participant's resting hour: mean 0.008 or smaller in magnitude against SDs of 1–182 — zero-centred, DC removed; perfusion index is unavailable, as in repo 1. Sample rates measured from the timestamps: BVP 64.0–64.0 Hz, ACC 32.0–32.0, EDA and TEMP 4.0–4.0. Dexcom strictly increasing in every file at a median interval of 300 s; **1 readings at the 40 mg/dL floor and 0 at the 400 ceiling across 36,898 readings** — censoring is not a factor in this cohort. E4 and Dexcom overlap 138–238 h per participant: the date shifts are consistent within each participant.
- **Three structural facts the description does not state, found and handled:** (1) **participant 015's E4 files are two recordings concatenated out of order** — every channel has one backward step, from 25 July 07:51 back to 5 July; the loader sorts each channel (stably, nothing dropped) and records the count (`reordered_steps`); the Dexcom file is clean, and after sorting 015 has 138 h of overlap but only 491 of 1,673 readings with a complete E4 window, because most of its E4 data precedes its Dexcom. (2) **Participant 001's HR file carries timestamps to the minute only** ("2/13/20 15:29", 60 samples per stamp; 623,610 duplicate stamps); the loader expands them to one-second offsets by file order and flags the channel; HR is Empatica-derived in any case. (3) **The food logs have three schemas**: the standard 14 columns; `time_of_day` for `time` in 007, 013, 015 and 016; and **003's file has no header and 11 columns**, lacking `time_end`, `dietary_fiber` and `total_fat` — its column order was inferred from the values (chicken nuggets 393 kcal / 19 g carbohydrate / 0.1 / 20; gumdrops 130 / 33 / 21 / 0: the trailing pair is sugar and protein) and is flagged as inferred. Whether the paper used the food log at all is protocol cell 2.
- **Also found:** PhysioNet's own download endpoints are throttled to ~35 KB/s per IP; the dataset came from PhysioNet's AWS S3 mirror of version 1.1.2, with **every file admitted only after its SHA-256 matched the 1.1.3 list** (two truncated transfers were re-fetched), and the four version-1.1.3 food logs from PhysioNet directly. 130 of 130 verified.

### D-006 · Coverage and the glucose distribution: a cohort that is almost never out of range
- **Date / commit:** 2026-10-20 · `2742ee6`
- **Status:** adopted
- **Result** (`results/coverage.csv`, `results/glucose_distribution.csv`): E4 worn 97–202 h per participant over spans of 158–476 h; Dexcom 1,673–2,847 readings per participant at 75–100% coverage; E4–Dexcom overlap 3078 h in all; **28,592 of 36,898 readings (77%) have a complete 10-minute E4 window preceding them** (the paper's own window length is a protocol cell; this is the usable-time count at a conservative length). **Glucose: mean of participant means 114.5 mg/dL, participant means 93–130, SDs 15–29; pooled, 0.57% of readings are below 70 mg/dL and 1.78% above 180; 1 at the sensor floor.** Six participants have no reading above 180 at all; ten have fewer than 1% below 70.
- **Why this is the central fact:** a per-participant mean is within one SD of almost every reading, so the baselines of D-004 are strong by construction, and any regression metric pooled over this cohort is dominated by the between-participant spread of means (93–130) rather than by within-participant excursions — exactly what a within-participant random split would let a model learn and read back (D-003). The narrowness is stated before the paper's claims are transcribed so it cannot be read as a post-hoc excuse.

### D-007 · Protocol cells 1–6 transcribed from the paper: what the claims are, what the inputs were, how the data were split
- **Date / commit:** 2026-10-25 · `65b8e7e`
- **Status:** adopted — each cell checked against the paper by Suhas on 2026-10-26, no changes (D-009)
- **Decision:** `docs/protocol.md` cells 1–6 are filled from the main text (Results "Glucose excursion classification" and "Glucose prediction", Methods "Feature engineering", "Classification of glucose excursions", "Glucose prediction"), Tables 1–3, Supplementary Table 1 (the 69 features, transcribed row by row into `docs/features.md`) and the Reporting Summary; each cell names where to look.
- **Evidence:** the two cells the week turns on. **Split (cell 5):** the classification is a repeated stratified 10-fold × 3 on windows, "not grouped by participant and not blocked in time" — adjacent five-minute windows of one participant sit on both sides of every fold; the population regression is leave-one-person-out; the personalised regression is first-half / second-half within participant. **Inputs (cell 2):** the food log is an input — 19 of the 69 features, 37.0 % of the paper's random-forest importance, sugar over 24 h the second most important feature — and so are HbA1c, sex and a participant-ID feature; **lagged glucose is never an input** ("all features used in modeling were historical (5 min to 24 h prior to the measurement being predicted)", none derived from CGM). The models are estimators of glucose from non-glucose inputs, not forecasters, which is why persistence is dropped from the baselines (D-009). The claims (cell 1): balanced accuracy 84.3 ± 1.3 % (Table 2 prints "0.013%"), 82.0 % on a 70/30 split, 52.0 % for logistic regression; RMSE 21.22 ± 4.14 (LOPO) and 21.10 ± 4.50 mg/dL (personalised), MAPE 14.33 and 13.26 %; the naive mean and median predictors are said to have been beaten and are given no number anywhere.
- **Two discrepancies found in transcribing, recorded not resolved:** `Demographics.csv` lists 9 female and 7 male participants where Supplementary Table 2 and the Reporting Summary say 8 and 8; the file is used as released. Supplementary Table 1's wake-time rule ("points > 2 → 1") and the Methods text ("when two of the four … a '0'") disagree on the threshold; the Methods text is followed (D-008 h).
- **Rejected:** filling any cell from memory of the paper, or from the brief's summary of it alone — every number was read back from the paper's own text or tables (`scratchpad` copies of the article, Tables 1–3 and both supplementary PDFs, not committed).

### D-008 · The underspecified choices, resolved from the DBDP code where it exists and decided where it does not
- **Date / commit:** 2026-10-25 · `65b8e7e`
- **Status:** adopted
- **Decision:** protocol cell 7 (a–m). The companion code holds the general functions the Methods cite, not the study's modelling scripts: `wearablecompute` `4e20fda` (first author's feature package: `e4import`, `HRV`, `SDNN`, `RMSSD`, `NNx`, `PeaksEDA`, `exercisepts`, `crhythm`), `Heart-Rate-Variability` `5d29f58`, `Model-Development/loocvRF.py` `6f8bfa4`. Read, cited, not copied.
- **Evidence — resolved from the code:** EDA peaks are `find_peaks(height=0, distance=4, prominence=0.3)` on the raw EDA (no filtering); the HRV metrics are statistics of the raw intervals in ms, SDNN with ddof = 1, NN50 the count of successive differences strictly above 50 ms, pNN50 in percent, RMSSD over all successive differences with no gap check; the `Heart-Rate-Variability` repository's version smooths the IBI with a 10-beat rolling mean first and is rejected because Supplementary Table 1's formulas are on the intervals themselves; activity bouts compare an epoch's ACC and HR means with the expanding mean of all earlier epochs (exclusive), so the first epoch is never a bout; minutes from midnight is 60 h + m + round(s/60) and hours from midnight is round(minutes/60), an integer; MAPE is mean |y − ŷ| / y × 100 per fold and the reported SD is `np.std` over folds; the random-forest feature selection is fitted on the training fold only.
- **Decided, because no code settles them:** (a) the epoch is the 5 minutes ending at the reading, [T − 5 min, T), every window trailing to T, nothing at or after T used; (b) an epoch is usable when ACC, EDA, TEMP and HR each have ≥ 1 sample in it (the code's `resample().mean()` behaviour), HRV is NaN below 2 IBI, XGBoost takes NaN natively, the decision tree and the linear baselines get NaN rows dropped and the count reported; (c) class balancing by random undersampling to the smallest class, seed 0, N reported beside 8,666 and not tuned towards it; (d) the 24-h label window is trailing, includes the current reading, ddof = 1, minimum 12 readings; (e) "first contiguous half" by count of usable epochs; (g, h) the wake-time proxy compares each quantity with its **trailing 24-h mean** rather than the calendar day's mean, because the day's mean reads samples after the target and §10 forbids it, and the "sharp change" is the day's first rising edge of the 3-h awake average confirmed at +25 and +75 min, the feature being the most recent wake time confirmed by T; (i) ID as the integer 1–16, sex MALE = 1; (j) the dataset's nutrient columns as logged, one eating event per distinct `time_begin`, 003's missing columns are none the paper's features use; (k) pandas conventions for SD and skew, ACC in g (a constant factor the models are invariant to).
- **Why these are decisions and not bugs:** each changes a feature's value, none changes what the feature is of; all are written before any number exists and the paper's numbers are compared against them as frozen. The wake-time and label-window choices are the two most likely to differ from the authors' and are the first thing to revisit if the classification under R1 misses by more than the brief's 10 points.

### D-009 · The pre-registered design and the predictions, frozen with the protocol (supersedes D-003 and D-004)
- **Date / commit:** 2026-10-25 · `65b8e7e`
- **Status:** adopted — frozen 2026-10-25 at the hash below; cells 1–7 verified against the paper by Suhas on 2026-10-26 with no changes, so the hash stands
- **Protocol hash:** `7860469db2e059bd` (SHA-256 prefix of `docs/protocol.md`, pinned by `tests/test_protocol.py`)
- **Decision:** protocol §8–§11, revised from the 20 October draft before the freeze. Baselines B0 population mean, **B1 per-participant mean from the training portion**, B2 HbA1c-and-sex linear model (LOPO), B3 per-participant linear model on the E4 features only — persistence is dropped because cell 2 established that no model uses lagged glucose, and B2 is added because HbA1c and sex are two of the 69 inputs carrying 10.8 % of the paper's importance (D-004 superseded). Regimes R1 the paper's, R2 participant-grouped k-fold, **R3 time-blocked within participant with no window within 24 h of the boundary on either side**, because the classification label is a 24-h rolling statistic (D-003 superseded: the exclusion zone is new). Ablations on the full regression model under R1 and R3: minus food log; minus sex, HbA1c and ID; E4 only; food log only.
- **Prediction** (verbatim from the brief, judged later against these words; any failure is reported as a failure; the write-up's first sentence is whichever of P1–P4 was decisive):
  - P1. The per-participant mean (B1) under the personalised regime lands within 3 mg/dL of the paper's 21.10; the personalised XGBoost beats B1 by less than 3 mg/dL. Rationale: Day 0 measured within-participant SD at 15–29 mg/dL.
  - P2. B0 or B2 under LOPO lands within 3 mg/dL of the paper's 21.22; the population XGBoost beats B2 by less than 3.
  - P3. The classification result reproduces within 3 points under R1 (84.3%) and falls by more than 15 points under R2 and R3. Rationale: adjacent 5-min windows share a participant, an hour and a rolling-24-h label.
  - P4. B1's "accuracy" (100 − MAPE) is above 84%, i.e. inside the paper's headline range, from a constant.
  - P5 (measurement, not prediction). Removing the food log costs more RMSE than removing all E4 features. Report which way it goes.
- **Stop conditions carried:** any feature using a CGM value or any sample at or after the target → bug (guarded by `tests/test_features.py`); any baseline computed with test-portion data → bug; any prediction edited after a number is seen → not permitted; classification under R1 more than 10 points from 84.3 % → stop and report before building on it.

### D-010 · The 69 features built on the Day 0 cache: the usable set is 28,964 epochs, all 69 present on 25,644 — the paper's ~25,000
- **Date / commit:** 2026-10-25 · `65b8e7e`
- **Status:** adopted
- **Result** (`results/epochs.csv`, `results/feature_missingness.csv`; `src/features/`, 18 synthetic tests in `tests/test_features.py`): under cell 7 b, **28,964 of 36,898 readings (78.5 %) have a usable epoch** — every one of ACC, EDA, TEMP and HR present in the five minutes before the reading. Day 0's stricter count (a gap-free 10-minute BVP window) was 28,592; the two differ by 372 because a five-minute epoch needs only one sample per channel. In every participant the four channels' missing counts are identical to within one epoch (e.g. 001: 499 each; 015: 1,176 each): an unusable epoch is the E4 being off, not a channel dropping out. Per participant the usable share runs 30 % (015, whose E4 mostly precedes its Dexcom, D-005) to 95 % (010, 013).
- **Missingness per feature:** 55 of the 69 features are never missing on a usable epoch. The eight HRV metrics are missing together on 8.9 % pooled (1.0–18.5 % per participant) — epochs with fewer than two Empatica IBI; `WakeTime` on 2.5 % pooled (1.0–28.8 %, the leading epochs before a day's first confirmed wake); the four skews and HR_Std on ≤ 1.9 % (a flat or one-sample epoch has no skew or SD). **All 69 present on 25,644 epochs (88.5 % of the usable set)** — the paper used "over 25,000"; the match is noted, not engineered: no threshold was moved towards it. Wake times found: 3–10 per participant (one per worn day, roughly). Eating events 24–83 per participant from 45–173 log rows; one row in 012 has no parseable time and is dropped; no row lacks a nutrient the features use (003's headerless file included).
- **What the first run found and fixed:** the wake-time feature came out NaN on every participant. The builder handed DataFrame columns (a positional index) to a frame built on the time index, and pandas aligned them to NaN; the synthetic tests had passed arrays. Fixed by converting to arrays inside `wake_time`, with a regression test that feeds Series. The rule itself, checked on 001 before the fix, behaves as intended: the awake proxy averages 0.00–0.03 between midnight and 04:00 and 0.5–0.8 from 06:00 to 10:00, with 89 confirmable rising edges over 9 days.
- **Guards in place:** `test_no_sample_at_or_after_the_target_changes_a_feature` multiplies and jitters every wearable sample and every food-log nutrient from T onwards and asserts the row at T (and the one before it) is bit-identical; `test_no_feature_depends_on_the_cgm_value` shifts every glucose value by 200 and asserts the 69 columns are unchanged. The half-record boundary for the personalised regime (cell 7 e) is recorded per participant in `results/epochs.csv`.

### D-011 · The labels reproduce the paper's Table 1; the balanced set is N = 10,230 against the paper's 8,666
- **Date / commit:** 2026-10-26 · `pending`
- **Status:** adopted
- **Result** (`results/labels.csv`, `results/label_distribution.csv`, `results/balanced_set.csv`; `src/labels.py`, tests in `tests/test_baselines.py`): under cell 7 d — trailing 24-h mean ± 1 SD of the participant's own CGM, current reading included, ddof = 1, at least 12 readings — **36,722 of 36,898 readings are labelled** (the first 11 of each record are not): PersLow 5,206 (14.2 %), PersNorm 25,854 (70.4 %), PersHigh 5,662 (15.4 %). **The class distributions land on Table 1:** PersNorm 111.8 ± 14.6 mg/dL, range 66–170 (paper 112.4 ± 14.5, 66–164); PersHigh 148.5 ± 25.7, 86–261 (149.9 ± 24.4, 93–260); PersLow 91.0 ± 11.6, 40–142 (90.8 ± 11.1, 46–120). Means within 1.4 mg/dL and SDs within 1.3 of the paper's on every class: the label definition and its window are the paper's to within what the data permit, and the PersLow floor of 40 says this dataset includes the one at-floor reading the paper's range (46) did not.
- **The balanced set** (cell 7 c): on the 25,644 complete labelled epochs (3,410 PersLow, 18,413 PersNorm, 3,821 PersHigh), undersampling every class to the smallest with seed 0 gives **N = 10,230** (3 × 3,410). The paper's 8,666 is not divisible by 3, so its rule was not this one; 8,666 / 3 ≈ 2,889 is close to a smallest class of ~2,900 on a set of ~25,000, so the difference is the size of the complete set, not the rule. Not tuned towards 8,666 (D-008 c). Per participant the balanced set holds 116 (015) to 946 (005) windows; the PersNorm share before balancing is 65–75 % in every participant, which is what the balanced accuracy conceals (prevalence-adjusted PPV on Wednesday, §10).

### D-012 · Baselines B0–B3 under every regression regime: a per-participant constant scores the paper's personalised RMSE; P1a, P2a and P4 held
- **Date / commit:** 2026-10-26 · `pending`
- **Status:** adopted
- **Result** (`results/baselines_summary.csv`, `results/baselines_per_participant.csv`, `results/predictions.csv`; `src/eval/baselines.py`, `src/splits.py`; the guard `test_no_baseline_reads_a_test_glucose` perturbs every test-portion glucose under all three regimes and asserts no prediction moves). RMSE mean ± SD over participants (np.std, as the paper's `loocvRF` reports it), bootstrap 95 % CI over 16 participants in brackets:
  - **R1 LOPO:** B0 population mean 22.47 ± 4.12 [20.44, 24.49], MAPE 15.20; **B2 linear on HbA1c + sex 21.47 ± 3.94 [19.49, 23.40], MAPE 14.41, "accuracy" 85.59** — the paper's population XGBoost: 21.22 ± 4.14, MAPE 14.33, accuracy 85.67.
  - **R1 halves:** B0 22.22 ± 5.65; **B1 per-participant mean 20.99 ± 4.17 [19.04, 23.08], MAPE 13.03, "accuracy" 86.97** — the paper's personalised XGBoost: 21.10 ± 4.50, MAPE 13.26, accuracy 86.74; B2 21.38 ± 4.69; **B3 per-participant ridge on the 45 E4 features 22.00 ± 4.66 on 13,096 complete rows, against B1 on the same rows 20.83 ± 4.18** — the wrist features add 1.2 mg/dL of error to a constant.
  - **R3 time-blocked** (first 70 % → last 30 %, 24-h exclusion on both sides; 16,057 train, 4,826 test, **8,081 epochs in the zones**; 015 has no test rows beyond its zone and is omitted, 15 participants): B0 22.77 ± 8.52; B1 21.15 ± 7.44 [17.63, 25.17]; B2 21.22 ± 7.52; B3 23.57 ± 7.73 against B1 on the same rows 20.98 ± 7.63. Same ordering, wider intervals. Clarke A + B is 98.9–99.4 % for every baseline under every regime: on a cohort that is 97.6 % inside 70–180, the grid cannot separate a constant from anything.
- **Verdicts, against the frozen words (D-009):** **P1a held** (B1 20.99 vs 21.10: within 0.11 mg/dL, criterion 3). **P2a held** (B2 21.47 vs 21.22: within 0.25; B0 22.47 within 1.25). **P4 held** (B1 "accuracy" 86.97 % > 84 %; it is in fact above the paper's own 86.74). P1b, P2b, P3 and P5 wait for the models and are marked pending in `results/predictions.csv`. No prediction's wording was touched.
- **What it means before any model exists:** the paper's headline regression numbers are the numbers a constant achieves on this cohort. Whether the XGBoost beats the constant at all, and by how much, is Tuesday's question (P1b, P2b), to be answered with paired per-participant deltas and the 16-participant bootstrap. The decision not to tune B3's ridge penalty (alpha = 1, chosen before any number) stands; a tuned B3 would still be compared against B1, not against the paper.
- **Decision carried into Tuesday:** every model is evaluated on exactly the rows and regimes above; paired deltas are computed against B1 (within-participant regimes) and B2 (LOPO) on identical rows; XGBoost takes NaN natively so its n is the full usable test set, and the paired comparison with B3 is restricted to B3's rows.
